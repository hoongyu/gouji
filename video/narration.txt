V1: Between 2013 and 2015, a man registered a company in Latvia with the same name as an Asian hardware supplier. He e-mailed two US internet companies as if he were that supplier. They wired him over a hundred and twenty million dollars. Nothing about it was technically clever. The name matched.
V2: Today, accounts-payable teams are handing invoices to AI agents. Those agents get fooled the same way, and they add a new weakness: text inside an invoice can tell them what to do. PayProof is an agent that investigates every payment request before any money moves.
V3: Here is an invoice from "Taivora Electronics". PayProof holds it: likely fraud, call the supplier on the number on file. Notice who decided: not the model, but a signed mandate. And the note hidden in the invoice, telling the AI to mark the supplier as verified, was caught by the Guard and quarantined. It is data, never an instruction.
V4: Behind that page, seven agents ran. The Planner picks checks. The Investigator runs each one as a single SQL query across five systems, the ERP, the vendor master, the contracts, the mailbox and the company registry, all federated through Zetaris, where the data already lives. This one query joins three of them, and shows the problem: the invoice comes from a Latvian company, while the order, the vendor master and the contract all point to Taiwan.
V5: Meterless's World Model resolves companies by registration number, not by name. So it sees three legal entities called Taivora, and raises the alarm. Every finding cites a cell, and the Verifier re-reads each one from its source before the brief may use it. Evidence counts as verified only when two independent trusted systems agree. The mailbox never counts: that is where the attacker writes.
V6: Even a bypass fails. A direct call to the payment system, without a contract, is refused. When everything checks out, like this legitimate bank change, the mandate allows it, and PayProof issues an execution contract: signed, bound to this exact payment, valid for fifteen minutes, usable once.
V7: Does it generalise? NVIDIA Nemotron wrote forty-four new requests, impostor e-mails, look-alike domains and new prompt injections, which we sealed before running them once. A payee list alone paid fifteen of twenty-four attacks. PayProof paid two, both on otherwise genuine invoices, and interrupted none of the twenty normal payments.
V8: Zetaris queries the data where it lives. Meterless gives us the World Model and the injected-generator pattern, and inspired the execution contract. NVIDIA Nemotron red-teams us. PayProof: an agent that can explain why, and that cannot pay without proof.
